Why Early Detection Matters

Chronic Kidney Disease often has no symptoms until 60–70% of kidney function is already lost. By that point, options are limited to dialysis or transplant.

Our tool helps you identify risk indicators early — swelling, fatigue, changes in urination, high blood pressure — so you can seek medical advice before it's too late.

About CKDDetect

CKDDetect is an academic machine learning project for early detection of Chronic Kidney Disease. Our system uses data from the Kaggle/UCI CKD dataset with 400 patient records and 24 clinical attributes.

Since our target users are the general public (without lab reports), we designed a smart symptom-to-clinical mapping layer that converts your answers to approximate clinical values, which are then fed to the trained ML model.

⚕️ Disclaimer: This tool is for educational purposes only and does not replace professional medical consultation.

What is Chronic Kidney Disease?

CKD is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for more than 3 months. It is staged by GFR (Glomerular Filtration Rate) from Stage 1 (mild) to Stage 5 (kidney failure). Early-stage CKD is asymptomatic, making screening critical for the general public.

GFR StagingNephrologySilent Disease

Key Symptoms & Risk Factors (General Public)

Observable warning signs include: persistent fatigue, swelling in feet/ankles (pedal edema), changes in urination frequency or colour, poor appetite/nausea, persistent back or side pain, and foamy urine. Risk factors include hypertension, diabetes, family history, obesity, and smoking.

Pedal EdemaFatigueHypertensionDiabetes

Symptom-to-Clinical Mapping Approach

Since the ML model was trained on clinical values (creatinine, hemoglobin, etc.), we built a heuristic mapping layer that translates symptom answers into approximate clinical ranges. For example: persistent fatigue + swelling + reduced urination → elevated serum creatinine + low hemoglobin + reduced packed cell volume. This allows general-public use without any lab tests.
